Revenue + Stablecoins: My Thesis for the 2026–2030 Crypto Bull Run

The next crypto cycle will favor protocols with measurable revenue, token-holder distributions, and stablecoins that function as global payment infrastructure.

Disclaimer: This is educational content, not financial advice. Crypto assets and public equities can lose substantial value. Verify figures and assess your own risk before acting.

Glossary of Terms

Stablecoin
A digital dollar issued on a computer network that keeps a steady value equal to one United States Dollar.
Blockchain Network
A shared, automated digital ledger that processes transactions and runs programs without needing a central bank.
Decentralized Exchange
An online trading venue operated by automated software code rather than a human broker.
Perpetual Futures
A financial contract that lets traders bet on whether an asset price will go up or down without an expiration date.
Token Buyback
When a crypto project uses cash profits to buy its own tokens from the open market and remove them from circulation.
Price-to-Earnings Ratio
A metric that compares a project's total market value against its annual net profits.
Fully Diluted Valuation
The total market value of a crypto project if every planned token were already circulating today.
Decentralized Lending Market
An automated financial pool where users deposit digital assets to earn interest while borrowers post collateral to take out loans.

The Meta Has Changed

My view for this market cycle is straightforward: tokens need revenue. Earlier crypto bull markets funded thousands of projects that produced no cash flow while issuing new tokens and diluting early buyers.

Crypto is becoming an operating industry. Investors are applying familiar measures such as revenue, operating margins, and cash returned to token holders. The strongest projects in this cycle will earn fees and pass cash to holders through token buybacks or yield payments.

Stablecoins form the second half of the thesis. Digital dollars have moved beyond trading pairs and now provide payment infrastructure for global commerce.

Why Tokens Are Starting to Work Like Stocks

Professional capital looks for cash flow. As traditional firms enter digital assets, they use metrics such as the price-to-earnings ratio to value projects.

When a project earns fees and uses code to buy its token on the open market, the buyback creates recurring demand. The mechanism resembles a public-company stock repurchase. Projects without profits or buybacks face dilution from future token unlocks.

On-Chain Profit Engines: My Top 5 Crypto Picks

Crypto fee revenue concentrates in perpetual trading, token creation, and decentralized lending.

01 · HYPE

Hyperliquid

Hyperliquid operates an automated trading venue for perpetual futures and spot markets. It earns fees from every trade. In the first half of 2026, it generated $419.3 million in fee revenue on $1.29 trillion in trading volume. Its fee routing sends 99% of non-developer trading fees to a vault that buys HYPE in the open market.

02 · SOL

Solana

Solana functions like a digital toll road: applications and users pay transaction fees to move funds and run programs. Applications on Solana earned more than $1.5 billion in total revenue during 2025, while the base network recorded $603 million in fee revenue. The network burns 100% of base transaction fees.

03 · PUMP

pump.fun

pump.fun collects trading and graduation fees when users launch and trade tokens. It generated $550 million in total revenue in 2025 and $227 million in fees during the first quarter of 2026. The project has directed treasury funds toward open-market PUMP buybacks.

04 · AAVE

Aave

Aave collects interest payments and liquidation fees from borrowers across its lending markets. It generated $141.8 million in protocol revenue in 2025 and has activated a token buyback program funded by protocol treasury revenue.

05 · ENA

Ethena

Ethena holds spot digital assets while shorting matching futures contracts, collecting funding yield during active markets. The system passes yield to stakers holding its digital dollar tokens.

Stablecoins Are Taking Over Global Settlement

Stablecoins have grown from trading tools into payment infrastructure. Total stablecoin transaction volume reached $33 trillion in 2025, up 72% year over year. Stablecoin supply now sits above $320 billion.

Reserve income

Issuers invest cash reserves in government debt and earn interest from the spread.

Payment rails

Stablecoins settle around the clock, across borders, with low transaction costs.

Corporate adoption

Visa and other networks are adding stablecoin settlement to existing payment systems.

Stablecoin issuers collectively hold more than $155 billion in United States Treasury bills. Tether reported more than $10 billion in net profit in 2025, and Circle reported $701 million in revenue and reserve income in the second quarter of 2026.

The Simple Math I Use to Evaluate Projects

1. Adjusted Price-to-Earnings Ratio

Market cap alone can hide developer payouts and token inflation. I compare fully diluted valuation with revenue after those costs:

Adjusted P/E = Fully Diluted Valuation ÷ (Gross Revenue − Developer Rewards − Token Inflation)

2. Net Buyback Rate vs. Token Unlocks

I compare the monthly tokens unlocked with the dollar value of buybacks converted into tokens:

Net Supply Change = Monthly Unlocks − (Buyback Value ÷ Token Price)

A negative result means buybacks absorb more tokens than the project unlocks.

Key Risks to Track

  • Vesting unlocks: Low circulating supply can create selling pressure when team or investor tokens vest.
  • Trading volume: Exchanges and token launch tools earn less during quiet markets.
  • Regulation: Regulators may scrutinize automated token buybacks that resemble dividends or share repurchases.

Bottom Line

My strategy for the 2026–2030 cycle is to follow verified fee cash flows and digital-dollar payment rails. Tokens with no revenue and high supply emissions face dilution. The projects best positioned for this cycle include trading exchanges, launch tools, lending markets, fast base chains, and public companies earning income from stablecoin reserves.
