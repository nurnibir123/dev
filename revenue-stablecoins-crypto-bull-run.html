Revenue + Stablecoins: My Thesis for the 2026–2030 Crypto Bull Run

The next crypto cycle will favor protocols with measurable revenue, token-holder distributions, and stablecoins that function as global payment infrastructure.

Glossary of Terms

Stablecoin
A digital dollar issued on a computer network that keeps a steady value equal to one United States Dollar.
Blockchain Network
A shared, automated digital ledger that processes transactions and runs programs without needing a central bank.
Decentralized Exchange
An online trading venue operated by automated software code rather than a human broker.
Perpetual Futures
A financial contract that lets traders bet on whether an asset price will go up or down without an expiration date.
Token Buyback
When a crypto project uses cash profits to buy its own tokens from the open market and remove them from circulation.
Price-to-Earnings Ratio
A metric that compares a project's total market value against its annual net profits.
Fully Diluted Valuation
The total market value of a crypto project if every planned token were already circulating today.
Decentralized Lending Market
An automated financial pool where users deposit digital assets to earn interest while borrowers post collateral to take out loans.

The Meta Has Changed

My view for this market cycle is straightforward: tokens need revenue. Earlier crypto bull markets funded thousands of projects that produced no cash flow while issuing new tokens and diluting early buyers.

The strongest crypto projects now look more like financial businesses than speculative experiments. Investors are applying familiar measures such as revenue, operating margins, and cash returned to token holders. The strongest projects in this cycle will earn fees and pass cash to holders through token buybacks or yield payments.

Stablecoins form the second half of the thesis. Digital dollars have moved beyond trading pairs and now provide payment infrastructure for global commerce.

Why Tokens Are Starting to Work Like Stocks

As institutional investors enter crypto, they are asking the same questions they ask of any business: Who pays, how much revenue does it produce, and who receives the profits? Traditional firms also use metrics such as the price-to-earnings ratio to value projects.

Token buybacks connect protocol activity to market demand: users pay fees, and the project uses part of that revenue to purchase tokens. The mechanism resembles a public-company stock repurchase. Projects that lack profits and buybacks leave holders exposed to dilution when new tokens enter circulation.

On-Chain Profit Engines: My Top 5 Crypto Picks

Crypto fee revenue concentrates in perpetual trading, token creation, and decentralized lending.

01 · HYPE

Hyperliquid

Hyperliquid is a decentralized exchange (DEX) for perpetual futures and spot markets. It earns fees from every trade. In the first half of 2026, it generated $419.3 million in fee revenue on $1.29 trillion in trading volume. Its fee routing sends 99% of non-developer trading fees to a vault that buys HYPE in the open market.

02 · SOL

Solana

Solana is a Layer 1 blockchain network that charges fees for transactions and application activity. It has also become a popular network for launching and trading memecoins, which drives additional transaction volume and fee revenue. Applications on Solana earned more than $1.5 billion in total revenue during 2025, while the base network recorded $603 million in fee revenue. The network burns 100% of base transaction fees.

03 · PUMP

pump.fun

pump.fun is a Solana-based launch platform where anyone can create a token with the click of a button and trade it. That low barrier has made it a popular home for memecoin launches. It generates revenue from token launches and trading activity, and it earned $550 million in total revenue in 2025 and $227 million in fees during the first quarter of 2026. The project has directed treasury funds toward open-market PUMP buybacks.

04 · AAVE

Aave

Aave is a decentralized lending platform where users can lend digital assets to earn interest or borrow against their collateral. It collects interest payments and liquidation fees from borrowers across its lending markets. It generated $141.8 million in protocol revenue in 2025 and has activated a token buyback program funded by protocol treasury revenue.

05 · ENA

Ethena

Ethena is a decentralized finance protocol that creates a digital dollar called USDe and backs its value with crypto assets and hedging strategies. The protocol generated $13.9 million in fees over the past 30 days.

Stablecoins Are Taking Over Global Settlement

Stablecoins have grown from trading tools into payment infrastructure. Total stablecoin transaction volume reached $33 trillion in 2025, up 72% year over year. Stablecoin supply now sits above $320 billion.

Reserve income

Issuers invest cash reserves in government debt and earn interest from the spread.

Payment rails

Stablecoins settle around the clock, across borders, with low transaction costs.

Corporate adoption

Visa and other networks are adding stablecoin settlement to existing payment systems.

Stablecoin issuers collectively hold more than $155 billion in United States Treasury bills. Tether reported more than $10 billion in net profit in 2025, and Circle reported $701 million in revenue and reserve income in the second quarter of 2026.

Pure-Play Infrastructure: My Top 5 Equity Picks

These public companies provide exposure to trading volume, stablecoin reserves, payment routing, and automated brokerage infrastructure.

Key Risks and What Would Prove Me Wrong

This thesis depends on fee revenue translating into durable holder value. I would reconsider it if the following signals appear:

  • Vesting unlocks: Low circulating supply can create selling pressure when team or investor tokens vest. I would want to see buybacks consistently absorb less new supply than projects release.
  • Trading volume: Exchanges and token launch tools earn less during quiet markets. Several quarters of falling fees, volume, and retained revenue would weaken the revenue thesis.
  • Stablecoin contraction: A sustained decline in stablecoin supply, transaction volume, or USDC circulation would challenge the payment-rails thesis.
  • Weak holder returns: If buybacks, burns, or staking yield fail to reach token holders while dilution continues, revenue alone will not support the thesis.
  • Regulation: New rules could restrict automated token buybacks, stablecoin reserve income, or the distribution of yield.

My invalidation checklist

I would change my view after a sustained period of falling protocol revenue, shrinking stablecoin usage, rising token supply, and weaker value transfer to holders. A single bad month would not settle the question. The trend across several quarters would.

Bottom Line

My strategy for the 2026–2030 cycle is to follow verified fee cash flows and digital-dollar payment rails. Tokens with no revenue and high supply emissions face dilution. The projects best positioned for this cycle include trading exchanges, launch tools, lending markets, fast base chains, and public companies earning income from stablecoin reserves.
